1

Lending Money to Family and Friends

Lending money seems like an act of generosity. In reality, it's one of the most destructive financial decisions you can make. Not only do you often lose the money, but you also destroy the relationship.

⚠️ What really happens when you lend money:

  • Most lent money is never fully recovered
  • Relationships often deteriorate or end
  • A dynamic of dependency and resentment is created
  • You lose control over your own wealth
  • The borrower rarely values the money the same way you do

✅ The solution:

If you want to help someone, gift what you can afford to lose without affecting your finances. If you can't gift it, don't lend it. Treat lent money as an expense, not an asset. And if you do decide to lend, do it with a written contract and a clear repayment plan. Friendship and money don't mix well.

2

Not Saving (or Saving Without Purpose)

Living without savings is living on the edge of the abyss. A single unexpected event — a repair, a fine, a medical emergency — can destabilize your entire life. But there's something worse than not saving: saving without a clear purpose.

⚠️ The consequences of having no savings:

  • Any emergency becomes high-interest debt
  • You live in constant financial stress, even if you earn well
  • You can't take advantage of investment opportunities
  • You're completely dependent on your next paycheck
  • You lose negotiating power in your job and your life

✅ The solution:

Build an emergency fund of at least 6 months of expenses. Then divide your savings into three categories: security (emergencies), opportunities (investments), and dreams (specific goals). Saving without purpose is as dangerous as not saving, because you end up spending it all on things without value.

3

Compulsive Spending (The Silent Killer)

It's not the big purchases that ruin you. It's the small, repeated, automatic ones. The daily coffee, the subscriptions you don't use, the impulse buys online, the unnecessary outings. Each one seems insignificant. Together, they're devastating.

⚠️ The real cost of compulsive spending:

  • Small daily expenses add up to thousands per year
  • Invested over decades, that money could become a fortune
  • Most of these expenses are completely automatic and unconscious
  • They generate a false sense of happiness that lasts minutes
  • You end up with nothing to show for years of spending

✅ The solution:

Apply the 72-hour rule: wait 3 days before any non-essential purchase. Cancel all subscriptions you haven't used in the last 30 days. Set a weekly cash budget for discretionary spending. And most importantly: make the opportunity cost visible. Every $100 spent today is money that could have grown for decades.

4

Not Protecting Your Money From Inflation

Keeping money "saved" in the bank isn't security. It's a guaranteed loss. Inflation silently reduces the purchasing power of your money every year. What's worth $100 today will be worth far less in 10 years if you do nothing.

⚠️ How inflation destroys your wealth:

  • At 5% annual inflation, your money loses half its value in 14 years
  • Traditional savings accounts pay less than 1% interest
  • Every year that passes without investing, you lose real purchasing power
  • "Safe" money in the bank is actually money losing value
  • Inflation isn't visible, but you feel it in every purchase you make

✅ The solution:

Don't leave large amounts of money in low-yield savings accounts. Learn to invest in assets that generate returns above inflation: index funds, real estate, businesses, or diversified financial instruments. The goal isn't just to save money, but to make it work for you and maintain its value over time.

5

Not Investing (Leaving Your Money Stagnant)

The most expensive mistake of all. Not investing isn't "being prudent." It's letting fear rob you of decades of growth. Money you don't invest is money that doesn't work for you. And lost time never comes back.

⚠️ The real cost of not investing:

  • Money invested over time grows exponentially through compound interest
  • If you wait years to start, you lose the most powerful growth period
  • Most people reach retirement without enough wealth
  • You'll depend on government pensions that are increasingly insufficient
  • The fear of losing makes you lose more than you imagine

✅ The solution:

You don't need to be a stock market expert. You need to start. Learn the fundamentals of investing: diversification, compound interest, and patience. Invest in low-cost index funds if you don't know where to begin. The key isn't perfect timing, it's time in the market. Every year you wait is a year of growth you lose forever.
