Calisthenics skills are not random tricks. They are the result of specific strength, technique and progressions. Follow the right steps and turn advanced movements into achievable targets.

The System

Every skill has a path.

The mistake most people make is trying to perform the final skill before building the abilities required for it. Instead, break the movement into smaller pieces and progressively remove assistance.

01 — Build Strength

Strength is the foundation. Your body needs enough force production to control the movement.

02 — Practice Technique

Strength alone is not enough. You need to learn the exact positions, coordination and movement pattern.

03 — Progress Gradually

Move through increasingly difficult variations instead of repeatedly failing the final skill.

SKILL 01

Muscle-Up

ADVANCED

The muscle-up combines a powerful pull with a transition and a strong dip. Train each component separately before putting them together.

1

Pull-Ups

Build a solid pulling base with strict pull-ups — 3–4 × 5–10.

2

Chest-to-Bar Pull-Ups

Pull higher than a normal pull-up. 3–4 × 4–8.

3

Explosive Pull-Ups

Pull as explosively as possible toward the upper chest. 4 × 3–5.

4

Straight-Bar Dips

Build the pushing strength needed after the transition. 3–4 × 6–12.

5

Band-Assisted Muscle-Ups

Practice the complete movement while gradually reducing assistance.

6

Negative Muscle-Ups

Start above the bar and slowly control the transition downward. 3–5 × 1–3.

7

Muscle-Up Attempts

Combine your explosive pull and transition. Keep attempts controlled and high quality.

Unlock Test: You should be able to perform strong chest-to-bar pull-ups, explosive high pulls and controlled straight-bar dips before expecting consistent muscle-ups.

Muscle-Up Session

Explosive Pull-Ups 4 × 3–5
Chest-to-Bar Pull-Ups 3 × 4–8
Straight-Bar Dips 3 × 6–12
Band Muscle-Up 3 × 3–5
SKILL 02

Front Lever

ADVANCED

The front lever requires enormous straight-arm pulling strength, scapular control and full-body tension.

1

Scapular Pull-Ups

Learn to control your shoulder blades. 3 × 8–12.

2

Tuck Front Lever

Bring your knees toward your chest and hold the body horizontal. 4 × 8–15 seconds.

3

Advanced Tuck

Open the hips and move the knees farther away from the chest. 4 × 8–15 seconds.

4

One-Leg Front Lever

Extend one leg while keeping the other tucked. Alternate sides.

5

Straddle Front Lever

Extend both legs outward while maintaining a horizontal body position.

6

Full Front Lever

Progressively close the legs until you can maintain a straight horizontal body.

Unlock Rule: Do not rush to the next progression. Aim to accumulate several clean sets of controlled holds before making the variation harder.

Front Lever Session

Scapular Pull-Ups 3 × 8–12
Front Lever Progression 4 × 8–15 sec
Tuck Front Lever Rows 3 × 5–8
Hollow Body Hold 3 × 20–40 sec
SKILL 03

Planche

VERY ADVANCED

The planche is primarily a straight-arm pushing skill. Your wrists, shoulders, chest, triceps and core all need to adapt progressively.

1

Pseudo Planche Push-Ups

Lean your shoulders forward over your hands while performing controlled push-ups. 3–4 × 5–10.

2

Planche Lean

Lock your elbows and gradually lean forward. 4 × 10–20 seconds.

3

Tuck Planche

Lift the feet and keep the knees tucked close to the chest. 4 × 5–12 seconds.

4

Advanced Tuck

Slowly open the hips while keeping the legs tucked.

5

Straddle Planche

Extend the legs outward to reduce the leverage requirement before attempting a full planche.

6

Full Planche

Gradually bring the legs together while maintaining straight arms and body control.

Important: Planche training places substantial stress on the wrists, elbows and shoulders. Progress slowly and stop if you experience sharp or persistent pain.

Planche Session

Planche Lean 4 × 10–20 sec
Pseudo Planche Push-Ups 3 × 5–10
Tuck Planche 4 × 5–12 sec
Hollow Body Hold 3 × 20–40 sec
SKILL 04

Handstand

INTERMEDIATE

The handstand is less about brute strength than many people think. You need shoulder mobility, balance, body alignment and enough pushing strength to maintain the position.

1

Pike Push-Ups

Build basic overhead pressing strength. 3–4 × 6–12.

2

Wall Handstand

Start with your feet against a wall and become comfortable supporting your body upside down. 3–5 × 20–40 sec.

3

Chest-to-Wall Handstand

Practice a straighter body position and active shoulders.

4

Heel Pulls

Briefly remove your feet from the wall and learn how to balance over your hands.

5

Freestanding Attempts

Practice controlled kick-ups and accumulate short, high-quality attempts.

Unlock Goal: The objective is not one lucky second. Build the ability to repeatedly control your balance and return to the position.

Handstand Session

Pike Push-Ups 3 × 6–12
Chest-to-Wall Handstand 4 × 20–40 sec
Heel Pulls 3 × 5–10
Freestanding Attempts 5–10 quality attempts
SKILL 05

L-Sit

BEGINNER → INTERMEDIATE

The L-sit is one of the best skills for developing compression, core strength, hip flexor strength and straight-arm support.

1

Support Hold

Press down through your hands and lift your body from the floor. 3 × 15–30 sec.

2

Tuck L-Sit

Bring your knees toward your chest while keeping your arms straight. 4 × 10–20 sec.

3

One-Leg L-Sit

Extend one leg while keeping the other tucked. Alternate sides.

4

Full L-Sit

Extend both legs and hold them parallel to the floor.

Unlock Test: When you can repeatedly hold your current progression with clean technique, increase the difficulty rather than simply adding endless hold time.

L-Sit Session

Support Hold 3 × 20–30 sec
Tuck L-Sit 4 × 10–20 sec
Compression Raises 3 × 8–15
Hollow Body Hold 3 × 20–40 sec
SKILL 06

Handstand Push-Up

ADVANCED

The handstand push-up combines the balance requirements of a handstand with substantial overhead pressing strength.

1

Pike Push-Ups

Start building vertical pressing strength. 3–4 × 6–12.

2

Elevated Pike Push-Ups

Elevate your feet to increase the shoulder-loading demand.

3

Wall Handstand Holds

Build the ability to support your body upside down.

4

Negative HSPU

Start at the top and slowly lower yourself. 3–5 × 1–5.

5

Partial ROM HSPU

Perform repetitions using a limited range of motion, gradually increasing depth.

6

Full HSPU

Progress toward controlled repetitions through your available range of motion.

Unlock Test: Your progression should be controlled. If you cannot control the negative phase, continue building strength before increasing the range of motion.
Programming

How to train skills

Skill training works best when your nervous system and muscles are relatively fresh. Put technical skill work near the beginning of your session rather than after exhausting yourself.

1. Warm Up

Prepare your wrists, shoulders, elbows and the specific joints involved in the skill.

2. Practice the Skill

Perform your current progression first while you are fresh. Prioritize quality over fatigue.

3. Build Strength

Follow the skill work with exercises that strengthen the exact muscles and positions required.

Front Lever Lever holds + pulling Straight-arm strength
Planche Leans + planche variations Straight-arm pushing

Progression Rules

When should you move up?

Do not change progressions simply because you are bored. Change them when the current variation becomes sufficiently controlled.

1

Own the Position

You can hold or perform the variation without losing the essential body position.

2

Control the Movement

You can control both the concentric and eccentric parts where applicable.

3

Repeat It

One lucky repetition does not mean you have unlocked the progression. You need repeatable performance.

4

Increase Difficulty

Extend the lever, reduce assistance, increase range of motion or move to the next progression.

Avoid These

Why people stay stuck

Skipping Prerequisites

Trying to muscle-up without enough pulling power or trying a planche without adequate straight-arm strength usually turns into poor technique.

Training to Failure

Constantly failing difficult skill attempts creates a lot of fatigue without necessarily producing better technique.

Changing Progressions Constantly

Give a progression enough time to develop instead of jumping between variations every workout.

Ignoring Recovery

Tendons and connective tissues can require substantial time to adapt, especially with straight-arm skills.

Only Practicing the Final Skill

The final movement is often too difficult to provide enough productive practice. Use easier variations.

Rushing the Timeline

Advanced calisthenics skills can take months or years to develop. Progress is more important than speed.
